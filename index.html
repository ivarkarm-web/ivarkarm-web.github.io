I don't chase perfection. I chase moments—where sound, place, and people line up.

Ivar Karm — Resonance

Foundational Principle

Before sound, there is listening.

I am a street performer, improviser, and handpan artist. The handpan is not merely an instrument — it is a wandering resonant chamber that mirrors wherever life places it.

Chapter I · Origins

Berlin (2008 – 2012)

Teenage years in Berlin, squatting forgotten spaces, playing inside S-Bahn viaducts and echoing metro tunnels. The cold industrial architecture taught me how overtones sustain in raw concrete.

Chapter II · The Open Road

The European Crossing (2013 – 2020)

Hitchhiking through the Netherlands, Belgium, Germany, Poland, Lithuania, Latvia, and Estonia with a handpan on my back. Sleeping under pines, playing in market squares, learning that music is the fastest bridge between strangers.

Chapter III · Current Horizon

The Aegean & Greece (Present)

Living under Mediterranean light. Handpans tuned to ancient scales, stone amphitheaters, coastal winds, and street sessions in Athens. Music created directly from presence.

Chapter 01 · Origins & Silence

Early Life & The First Spark

Found music in the quiet spaces between sounds — the wind through pine branches, rain striking rusty metal, the acoustic cadence of solitary walking.

Estonia · Childhood

Grew up surrounded by dense Baltic forests and profound silence. Learned that quietness is not the absence of sound, but the foundation upon which listening begins.

Limitation As Teacher

First instrument: a broken guitar with three nylon strings. Limitation isn't a barrier — it's an aesthetic direction. Each missing string forced deeper attention to what remained.

The Departure

Packed a modest canvas bag with spare clothes, a notebook, and that three-string guitar. No set destination in mind — just the absolute certainty that staying still was no longer viable.

Chapter 02 · The Resonant Vessel

How Sound Found Its Form

The street does not ask for credentials or diplomas. It demands only one thing: total, uncompromising presence with the instrument.

First Encounter · Berlin

Heard it under an arching concrete bridge in a Berlin park. A lone busker was coaxing celestial tones from a steel sculpture. I stood motionless for an hour, captivated. I knew then I had to dedicate my life to that vibration.

Street Tuition

No formal conservatories, no sheet music. Just thousands of hours on wind-blown street corners, underground metro platforms, and public squares. Every stranger who paused became a subtle teacher of resonance.

The Core Philosophy

True improvisation is not about demonstrating technical acrobatics; it is an act of communion. When you align your breath, the steel tone field, and the ambient environment, the music plays itself.

Chapter 03 · Street Chronicles

Moments of Pure Public Grace

Unrehearsed encounters in the subterranean arteries and sunlit squares of Europe, where anonymous commuters become accidental companions in sound.

“
Alexanderplatz, 3 AM

Playing into the echoing cavern of empty concrete. A solitary taxi driver parked on the curb, stepped into the midnight chill, and stood motionless for twenty minutes with his hands buried in his coat pockets. When the final harmonic decayed, he pressed a warm, folded banknote into my palm without uttering a single syllable.

“
Paris Metro · Line 4

A young woman was weeping quietly on a bench across the tiled platform. I softened my touch, coaxing the handpan into an ambient, cyclical lullaby — wrapping the cold tunnel in warmth. She gently dried her eyes and remained until her train arrived. Sound speaks where language is inadequate.

Athens Square

An elderly gentleman showed up every Tuesday afternoon. After six months, he brought his grandson, who unclasped a wooden folk flute. Without rehearsals, we jammed under the Acropolis till dusk.

Winter In Amsterdam

Numb fingers, biting sleet, empty canals. Kept playing anyway. A neighborhood baker hurried outside with freshly baked bread and steaming tea: “Music shouldn’t freeze because the city is cold.”

Chapter 05 · Acoustic Arsenal

Other Voices of Resonance

The handpan is my centerpiece, but each instrument in my travel bag unlocks a distinct acoustic frequency and cultural memory.

筝

Japanese Koto

Thirteen silk-wound strings over movable bridges. Tuned to pentatonic scales that echo water ripples across stone gardens.

🥁

Peruvian Cajón

The resonant wooden crate that anchors open-air improvisations with punchy bass booms and crisp snare clacks.

🪘

African Djembes

Goatskin head stretched over carved hardwood. Connects street listeners directly to primal human heartbeat rhythms.

🎵

Jew Harps (Dan Moi)

Plucked brass reeds where the player’s mouth and cranial cavities act as dynamic acoustic modulation chambers.

🎶

Diatonic Harmonicas

Pocket wind vessels shaped purely by human breath, bending plaintive minor chords on nocturnal railway bridges.

Chapter 07 · Aphorisms & Support

Notes from the Open Road

Reflections distilled from sixteen years of wandering with a steel handpan in hand.

  • Every scale I learn on the handpan began as a solitary walk through an unfamiliar city.
  • If you pause for three seconds while walking past, you are no longer an observer — you are part of the composition.
